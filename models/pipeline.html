1. The whole pipeline

Reading the diagram

Green gates are scripts: they measure and give the same answer every time. The orange gate is the only one that uses opinion (fresh AI judges), so it is the only one that can pass a model on one draw of judges and fail it on another. Everything before it is cheap; everything from it on costs judge time and run budget.

3. How the judge panel decides

Each judge scores every criterion 1–5 from the renders. A script (judge_verdict.py), not the agent, turns the scores into a verdict. Click a score to change it and watch the verdict move.

Why medians?

With an odd panel, the median is the middle judge. One judge who loves or hates the model can't move a criterion by themselves; two can.

Floor vs bar

Floor: no single criterion may be worse than this. Bar: the average of the medians must reach this. A kind with no bar is judged on the floor alone.

The golden anchor

Judges also see the kind's golden (a reference model) with its scores, and grade relative to it: "equal quality = equal score". The bar was set from the golden's own scores.

Fix notes

Every judge also lists fixes, each tagged with the criterion it raises. The next build must take on a lowest-median criterion (ledger.py plan). Fixes tagged placement go to habitat.js, not the model.

4. Budget and loops

What counts as a round

A judge round is one build that got judged. Polish rounds are rebuilds after a pass, to raise quality; the run keeps the best passing build (ledger.py best). Retries after a fail use the same loop but can't stop early.
